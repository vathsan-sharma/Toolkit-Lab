How to Count Words and Estimate Reading Time

Word limits show up everywhere: school essays, job applications, article briefs and social posts. Knowing what a word counter actually measures helps you hit the target.

Ad space

What counts as a word

Most word counters split text on spaces and line breaks, then count the pieces. That means "well-known" counts as one word, while "a - b" may count as three. Numbers such as "2026" are usually counted as words too. Different tools handle hyphens, contractions and symbols in slightly different ways, so a small difference between two counters is normal.

Characters with and without spaces

Character limits matter for titles, meta descriptions, tweets and form fields. Some platforms count spaces and others do not, so check the rule before you start. A typical meta description should stay around 150 to 160 characters including spaces.

Sentences and paragraphs

Sentence counts are useful for checking readability. A sentence ends with a full stop, question mark or exclamation mark. Long sentences, more than about 25 words on average, are harder to follow. Paragraphs are usually separated by a blank line.

How reading time is estimated

Adults typically read silently at about 200 to 250 words per minute, and faster readers manage more. Reading time estimates usually use 200 words per minute, which is a conservative figure. A 1,000-word article therefore shows as about five minutes. Real reading speed depends on the subject and the reader, so treat the figure as a rough guide.
